Email in the Overview
Five fine-grained proposals
Author: Consular · for Filippo
Status: for decision

Email belongs in the Overview. The only question is which shape.

The CRM already runs WhatsApp end to end: inbound matched to a lead, an activity feed, a Consular that drafts in Filippo's voice, an outbox that sends. Email is the missing second channel. This document proposes five distinct ways to bring it into the Overview, each fully integrated with the surfaces that already exist, each with a different organizing idea about what email is. One of them is the recommendation; all five are real.

00 / Status quo
The surfaces email has to plug into.

No proposal is judged in a vacuum. Each one is judged by how cleanly it sits on top of the machinery the CRM already runs. Here is that machinery, named, so every integration claim below can be checked against something real.

SURFACE 01

The Overview

The daily home screen. A time-of-day greeting, then buckets: overdue, due today, stuck in stage, unmatched WhatsApp. A single SQL pass at open. The opening is a statement plus an arrow, not a menu.

SURFACE 02

The Flight Deck

The strip above the chat. 1 to 6 picks written to db/flight-deck.json, ranked by ROI-per-minute, rendered as cards (hero, arrears, agenda, soft, gauge, tag). Each card carries a verb, channel, why, duration, deadline, tone.

SURFACE 03

The activity feed

The activities table. Types already include E-mail In, E-mail Out, WhatsApp In/Out, Call, Meeting, Note. Fields for message id, delivery status, media. Email is half-modelled already.

SURFACE 04

The WhatsApp pipeline

Baileys runtime: inbound matched to a lead by phone, the unmatched ones surfaced, outbound sent and status-tracked (sent / delivered / read). The pattern email should mirror.

SURFACE 05

The Consular

Drafts in Filippo's voice from the corpus. Classifies inbound (warm / ambiguous / cold), picks a trust level, proofreads, issues verdicts. Already speaks per contact-role.

SURFACE 06

The outbox

Scheduled sends with a scheduled_for timestamp, and the ten-minute countdown that lets a queued reply be pulled back before it leaves.

SURFACE 07

Prospects

Stage (Incoming → Closing), status (incl. Awaiting reply), next_action_date / label, amount_chf, confidence_pct, and intel_json with the verdict and lamps.

SURFACE 08

Contacts roster

1-to-many per lead with roles: champion, gatekeeper, blocker, decision maker. The draft changes with who receives it. Today matched by phone; email adds a second identity key.

SURFACE 09

Event-sourced store

Log-first, then SQLite projection. Every write is an event; the log is the truth and replay rebuilds the cache. Any email write obeys the same invariant.

01 / Foundation
What all five share, and what they do not.

Three things are common to every proposal, so they are settled once here and not re-argued five times.

1. The schema is already most of the way there. activities carries E-mail In and E-mail Out as first-class types with status and media fields. What is missing is not the data model, it is the transport: a way for mail to arrive and leave. Transport is a separate decision (Gmail API two-way is the leading candidate, because email from a relationship-selling rep must come from his real inbox and identity, not a crm@ sending domain). The five proposals below are about shape in the Overview, not transport. Any of them runs on any transport.

2. One new column, the rest is composition. Email and WhatsApp differ in cadence (email is slower, longer, threaded, often to a named role) but they are the same primitive: a message, on a channel, to a contact, about a deal. A single channel field on activities turns the existing feed bi-channel. Whether that field is surfaced as one merged stream or two parallel lanes is precisely what separates the proposals.

3. The Consular is the multiplier in all five. The reason to put email inside the CRM rather than leave it in Gmail is that the Consular can read the thread, know the deal, know the contact's role, and draft the reply in Filippo's voice. Every proposal leans on that. They differ in how visible the drafting is and where it sits.

Where they genuinely diverge is the organizing idea: is email an inbox, a triage queue, a draft pile, a set of ranked cards, or a deal-health signal? That choice cascades into the entire Overview UX. That is the real decision in front of you.

PROPOSAL AOne Thread

Email is not a new screen. It is a new bubble in the conversation you already have. WhatsApp and email merge into one per-lead thread, and the Overview stops caring which channel a message arrived on.

The core idea

Today a lead has a WhatsApp thread and, separately, email scattered in Gmail. Proposal A collapses that: a lead has one conversation, and each message is tagged with the channel it travelled on. The unmatched WhatsApp bucket becomes unmatched inbound, holding both channels. A lead detail shows a single chronological timeline with green bubbles for WhatsApp and blue for email. The rep stops thinking in inboxes and starts thinking in relationships, which is how a good salesperson already thinks.

Features
  • Channel-tagged unified timeline. One stream per lead, every entry stamped WhatsApp or Email, ordered by time, not by channel.
  • Identity merge. A contact's email address joins their phone as a matching key in the contacts roster, so a reply from marco@bistrot.ch lands on the same person as the WhatsApp number.
  • Channel-agnostic compose. The Consular drafts once; the rep picks the send channel at the last moment. The draft register adjusts: longer and signed for email, short for WhatsApp.

  • Cross-channel context for the Consular. When drafting, the Consular sees the whole thread, so it will not email a question the lead already answered on WhatsApp.

PROPOSAL BThe Triage Lane

Email has its own rhythm: slower, longer, threaded, often to a named decision-maker. Do not merge it, give it lanes. Email gets dedicated Overview buckets and a triage-first workflow beside the existing ones.

The core idea

Where A blends, B separates on purpose. Email is treated as a distinct workflow with its own buckets in the Overview, sitting next to the WhatsApp and stage buckets. The argument is honesty: email and WhatsApp are not the same act. A WhatsApp ping is a tap; an email is a letter, often to a gatekeeper or a buyer who took days to reply. Collapsing them, as A does, flattens a real difference. B keeps the difference and turns it into a clean triage rhythm: open the Overview, clear the email lane, clear the WhatsApp lane, move on.

Features
  • Four email buckets: Unmatched, Awaiting reply, Drafted-ready, Gone-quiet. Each is a queue with bulk actions.
  • SLA timers. A client email unanswered past a threshold (say 24h) climbs the bucket and turns amber then red, so nothing rots.
  • Inbound classification reused. The EVAL-INBOUND machinery (warm / ambiguous / cold + trust level) runs on email exactly as on WhatsApp, no new classifier.
  • Bulk triage. Select-all-cold, archive; select warm, send-to-Consular for drafts.
  • Lane independence. A noisy email day does not bury the WhatsApp lane and vice versa; each has its own count and rhythm.

PROPOSAL CConsular Co-Pilot

The point of email-in-CRM is not an inbox. It is that the replies are already written when you arrive. The Overview leads with a queue of Consular-drafted answers, not with raw mail.

The core idea

A and B both still ask the rep to read mail and decide. C inverts the work. Every inbound email immediately triggers the Consular to draft a reply, voice-matched, aware of the deal stage and the contact's role. By the time Filippo opens the Overview, the headline is not "12 emails," it is "12 in, 9 already answered for you, 3 need a human." Raw, undrafted email is the exception the system flags, not the default the rep wades through. This is the proposal most aligned with the entire reason the Consular exists: volume that outgrew one human, handled in his voice.

Features
  • Auto-draft on arrival. Inbound email fires a Consular draft immediately, with the thread, the lead's intel_json, and the contact's role as context.
  • Role-aware register. A reply to the champion opens warm; to the gatekeeper, brief and routing; to the blocker, never a pitch. The roster already encodes this.
  • Proofread gate. Every draft runs the PROOFREAD + VERDICT pass before it reaches the queue; anything with a fact-not-in-lore or a banned phrase is held with a reason.
  • Trust-zone auto-send. For low-stakes, high-trust replies (a "danke, passt"), the same trust-level mechanism that auto-sends WhatsApp can auto-send email, with the ten-minute pull-back.
  • One-tap review. Approve, edit-in-place, or snooze. The rep's job shrinks to judgment, not composition.

PROPOSAL DFlight Deck Native

Email does not need a surface of its own. Email actions become Flight Deck cards, ranked by ROI-per-minute next to calls and WhatsApp. Email earns attention on merit or it waits.

The core idea

The Flight Deck already answers "what should I do right now" with 1 to 6 ranked picks. D says email is just more input to that ranker, not a parallel world. A hot client email that says "can we sign this week" is a higher ROI-per-minute move than a cold WhatsApp nudge, so it should out-rank it on the same deck, as a REPLY card with a ▸ DRAFT action. A proposal emailed seven days ago with no reply is an arrears-style chase card. This is the proposal most faithful to the prioritization philosophy already in the product: do not show the rep an inbox, show him the next best move, regardless of channel.

  • Email feeds the ranker. Inbound and stalled emails become candidate cards, scored by ROI-per-minute exactly like every other move.
  • Channel drives the action. channel: "email" renders a ▸ DRAFT button that opens a Consular email draft; phone renders ▸ CALL.
  • Reuses card archetypes. arrears for email dunning (proposal sent → reminder → final), agenda for a scheduled email follow-up, tag for a quick reply.
  • Overflow chip. The deck stays curated at 1 to 6; the rest sit behind the queueCount chip, so high email volume does not break the deck.
  • One file, no new surface. Everything flows through db/flight-deck.json and the archetypes in flight-deck.jsx.

PROPOSAL EDeal-Health Signal

Email matters only as evidence of where a deal stands. Do not build an inbox, build deal-health detection. The Overview surfaces deals whose email state is a risk, not a list of messages.

The core idea

The most radical reframe. E argues that a rep does not want an email client at all, he wants to know which deals are slipping. So email is consumed for signal and thrown away as a surface. "Proposal emailed seven days ago, opened twice, no reply" is a stalling deal; "champion went warm then silent" is a cooling one. Those become entries in the Overview's risk panel and drive confidence_pct and next_action automatically. The Consular proposes the nudge when a thread cools. Reading and writing full threads still happens in Gmail; the CRM's job is to watch the pulse and tell the rep where to push.

Features
  • Email state as a deal vital. Sent / opened / replied / silent feeds confidence_pct and flips status to "Awaiting reply" automatically.
  • Stall detection. An emailed proposal with opens but no reply past N days surfaces as a risk with a named cause.
  • Cooling detection. A warm thread that goes quiet drops confidence and prompts a Consular-drafted nudge.
  • Lore capture. Email-derived signals ("opened the offer 3 times, never replied") write to intel_json so the next turn inherits them.
  • No inbox to maintain. The CRM never becomes a mail client; it stays a pipeline instrument.

08 / The call
Recommendation.
Recommendation
Ship D as the surface and C as the engine, on the channel-agnostic activities model. Keep B's lane as the overflow, not the default.

The honest synthesis is not one proposal, it is two that fit together and one held in reserve. D (Flight Deck Native) is the right surface because it is faithful to the philosophy already in the product and costs the least: email earns attention by value on the deck the rep already reads first, no new screen to learn. C (Consular Co-Pilot) is the right engine underneath it, because the entire reason to pull email into the CRM is that the Consular drafts the reply before the rep arrives. A deck card that says REPLY should open a draft that is already written, not a blank box.

Hold B (the Triage Lane) in reserve as the overflow surface for the days when email volume exceeds what a 1-to-6 deck can hold. It reuses the same buckets-and-eval machinery, so it is cheap to add later and does not need to ship first. A (One Thread) is the eventual destination once identity-merge is trustworthy, but its data risk means it should not lead. E (Deal Signal) is a powerful layer to fold into intel_json over time, not a v1 surface.

PHASE 1
Transport + model. Gmail API two-way (his real inbox, his identity). Add channel to activities and the outbox. Inbound matched to a lead by from-address, logged as E-mail In. This is CD-specced, Code-wired.
PHASE 2
Engine (C). Inbound email auto-fires a Consular draft, role-aware, proofread-gated, into the outbox queue with the ten-minute pull-back.
PHASE 3
Surface (D). Email moves become Flight Deck cards: REPLY, CHASE, SEND, ranked by ROI-per-minute, ▸ DRAFT opens the pre-written reply.
PHASE 4
Overflow (B) + signal (E). Add the email triage lane for high-volume days, and begin folding email pulse into confidence_pct and the lore.
09 / Appendix
Data model and the one new column.

What actually changes in the store. The point of the appendix is to show the build is small: the schema already speaks email, this adds a channel discriminator and a transport.

The activities table already carries the email types and the status and media fields. The single structural addition is a channel discriminator, written event-first like every other mutation so replay rebuilds it.

// activities: the one new column, everything else exists
ALTER TABLE activities ADD COLUMN channel TEXT DEFAULT 'whatsapp';
// values: 'whatsapp' | 'email' | 'call' | 'note'
// E-mail In / E-mail Out types already exist; channel just makes
// the feed and the buckets queryable per channel.

// outbox row, channel-agnostic
{
  prospect_id: 'P033',
  channel:     'email',        // drives transport at send
  to:          'silke.lengler@gmail.com',
  subject:     '...',
  body:        '...',            // Consular draft, voice-matched
  scheduled_for: '2026-06-14T18:10:00Z', // 10-min pull-back
  drafted_by:  'consular',
  status:      'queued'          // queued|sent|delivered|opened|replied
}

// transport: Gmail API two-way (Phase 1)
inbound:  watch → match from-address to contact → appendEvent(E-mail In)
outbound: outbox row → Gmail API send → appendEvent(E-mail Out, threadId)
// log-first, then SQLite projection. Same [SYNC-L] invariant as WhatsApp.

Identity matching (the cost centre for Proposal A) lives in contacts: a contact gains an email key beside phone, and inbound matching tries email first, then domain, then falls to the unmatched bucket, exactly as unmatched WhatsApp works today. Nothing here is a new subsystem. It is the WhatsApp pattern, run a second time, on a second transport, surfaced through the deck the rep already reads.

10 / Decisions
What I need from you before CD specs the build.
  1. Surface choice. Confirm the recommendation (D surface + C engine, B in reserve), or pick a different lead proposal. This is the one decision that scopes everything else.
  2. Transport. Gmail API two-way (your real inbox, recommended) versus a transactional provider (a crm@ identity). For relationship selling I read this as Gmail, but it is your call.
  3. Auto-send appetite. Should the trust-zone auto-send that exists for WhatsApp extend to low-stakes email, or must every email be human-approved at first? Email is higher-stakes, so I lean human-approved until trust is earned.
  4. Open-tracking. Proposal E and the "opened 3x" signals need read-tracking pixels. Useful, but some clients and privacy norms dislike them. In or out for v1.
  5. Identity merge timing. Ship channels as parallel lanes first (B/D), or invest in the contact identity-merge now so One Thread (A) is reachable sooner.

Pick a lead proposal and answer the five, and I will write the spec to CD, who specs the build for Jedi Code to wire. I propose, they build. Nothing here touches the database without your sign-off on the surface first.
